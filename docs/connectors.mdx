A connector is an MCP server Mobster's agent can use during a task. With Linear connected, "Find the login bug in Linear and text Sam its title" reads the issue from Linear and sends the text on your iPhone, in one task.

Connectors are part of Mobster for Mac. The `mobster` command doesn't use them, and a task another agent starts over [MCP](/mcp-server) or [Mobster Remote](/remote) never gets them. One exception: [Mobster Checks](/ci/github-app) uses your GitHub connector's sign-in to confirm you can push to a repository before `mobster test pr` posts there.

## The Connectors page

**Connectors** is one page for every outside service Mobster works with. Open it with **Connectors** in ⌘K, **Connectors** in Settings (⌘,), or **Connect a tool** in the task box's **+** menu. It has no row in the sidebar. A service that connects through Mobster's own app, such as GitHub, needs your Mobster account: without it, **Connect** says so, with **Sign in to Mobster**, before any browser opens. A service Mobster doesn't offer yet, such as Slack while Mobster's own Slack app is in testing, is named under the directory, and searching for it says why. **Your connectors** lead it: each service you connected is one row, with what it can do: **Use in tasks** (tools Mobster's agent calls during a task, this page) and **Send results** (where a finished task's result goes, [Send results to other tools](/webhooks)). A service that does both, such as Notion, is one row with both. While its tools answer, the row has **Use in a task**, which opens a new task with the service's first example (or `Use Notion to `) for you to finish, and sends nothing. The directory follows, and the page ends with **Use Mobster from…** ([Use Mobster from Claude and ChatGPT](#use-mobster-from-claude-and-chatgpt)).

Each connector's row says how its connection stands, in words and with a dot:

| Row says | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| `23 tools. 2 run without asking.`, green dot | A session is open and answers now | Nothing |
| `23 tools. Each asks you first.` | No session is open. Mobster connects when a task needs it | Nothing, or **Test** |
| `Connecting…` | Testing or reconnecting | Wait |
| `Finish in your browser. Mobster picks up here when you're done.` | The service's sign-in page is open in your browser | Sign in there, or **Open the page again** if you closed it. **Cancel** removes a connector that has never signed in |
| `Not connected.`, or why the first sign-in didn't finish, such as `You didn't allow Mobster to connect.` | It has never signed in | **Connect**, or **Remove** |
| `Signed out by the service. Connect to sign in again.`, amber dot | The service stopped taking your sign-in and Mobster couldn't refresh it | **Connect** |
| Why it failed, such as `Nothing answered at 127.0.0.1:9. Check the address, and that the server is running.` or `Mobster couldn't find mcp.exmaple.com. Check the address.`, in coral | The last connect or call didn't work | **Reconnect** when the server is back, or **Edit** your own server's address |

**Reconnect** closes the session, connects again, checks the server with an MCP `ping`, reads its tools and keeps the session open.

On the Connectors page, **Details** on a row opens that service's page. While its sign-in is open in your browser, the page shows Mobster's icon and the service's joined by dots that pulse toward the service, with **Open the page again** and **Cancel**. When you're back from the browser, the dots meet at a green check, `Connected to Linear` shows in a notice, and the page lists the tools and what each may do. A sign-in that didn't finish breaks the line; one that worked before and now fails marks it in coral, with **Connect** or **Reconnect**.

## Add a connector

The directory is on the Connectors page, under your connectors. **Search**, beside the page's title, narrows the directory and your connectors by name. **Your own server**, and Import from Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Cursor or Codex, are rows under **Build and test apps**, and stay there after you connect every service it lists. Each service is a row with what it does. A check beside the name means Mobster made it and tests it with every release: Webhook, Folder, Email and [GitHub checks](/ci/github-app). A row says who makes the service when that isn't the service itself, such as **by Atlassian** under Jira, and has no check. Click **Connect** on its right to connect it: its page opens and the sign-in starts there. A service that can both be used in tasks and receive results asks which. The rows are in three sections: **Build and test apps**, **Plan content and client work**, and **Send results to**. Click a row to see how connecting works, what it can do, its access, who makes it, how you sign in and its server address before you connect. **Try asking** on that page lists tasks to try with it; clicking one opens a new task with those words, and sends nothing.

The directory lists a gated connector only after it passes sign-in, a read, a write when supported, disconnect and reconnect. Slack, Google Drive, GitLab, Make, monday.com, Microsoft 365 and Bitrise stay hidden until they pass. A connector you already added keeps working.

The directory lists a service once Mobster has tested it for real: it connects, reads in a task, and writes where the service can. A service in this table that the directory doesn't show yet works under [Your own server](#add-your-own-server), with the address the table gives.

Each server is its vendor's own, at the address the vendor publishes. For most, **+** opens the vendor's sign-in page in your browser, the vendor registers Mobster as a client there, and you allow it. Nothing is shared until you do. After you sign in, Mobster tests the connection and lists the server's tools, such as `Connected. 23 tools.`

### Mobster's own Slack, Google and GitHub apps

Slack, Google Drive and GitHub let no app register itself, so Mobster signs you in with an OAuth app of its own at each. The app's secret stays on Mobster's server, `api.mobster.dev`, which trades the sign-in code for your tokens and refreshes them. Your tokens go to your Mac's Keychain. The server keeps no code or token.

Mobster's own apps work while your Mobster account has a plan or a trial: your Mac sends your account's token with each sign-in and refresh, and only to `api.mobster.dev`. Without a plan or trial, or on a Mobster from before this, the sign-in stops with `Mobster's own Slack app needs your Mobster account, with a plan or trial in force, on the latest Mobster. Update Mobster and sign in, then connect Slack again.` Your own app or token works without an account.

Click **+**, allow Mobster on the service's page in your browser, and Mobster comes to the front with the connector connected. The browser tab shows Mobster's icon and Slack's joined by a dotted line with a check, says `Connected to Slack`, and that you can close it. Your browser may ask once whether it can open Mobster. If you don't allow it, the line is broken and the tab says `Slack isn’t connected`; when the sign-in fails, it says why.

- Each service sends your browser back to `api.mobster.dev`, which passes the code straight to Mobster on your Mac. If you press **Cancel** on the service's page, Mobster says `You didn't allow Mobster to connect.` and **Connect** starts again.
- Slack's tools run on Slack's Web API, with your sign-in: Slack's MCP server takes only apps listed in the Slack Marketplace, and the Web API takes any app your workspace installed.
- Mobster's Google app asks Google for one scope, `drive.file`: Mobster sees only the Google Drive files you use with Mobster, not the rest of your Drive. The Google Drive connector and the Google Sheets destination use the same app.

When Mobster's app can't sign you in, **+** opens a sheet that says why, such as `Mobster's own Google Drive app isn't set up yet.` **Use your own app** (for GitHub, **Use your own token**) shows the fields:

- **Slack** and **Google Drive**: create an OAuth app at the vendor ([Slack's MCP docs](https://docs.slack.dev/ai/mcp-server/), [Google's setup](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/configure-mcp-server), which needs Google's Workspace Developer Preview), add `http://127.0.0.1:47613/callback` to its redirect URLs, and paste its client ID and secret. Signing in with your own app needs port 47613 free on your Mac. Mobster asks your Google app for `drive.file` alone, so add that scope to its Data Access.
- **GitHub**: paste a [personal access token](https://github.com/settings/personal-access-tokens).

A connector that has your own app or token keeps using it.

The Slack connector's sign-in is the only Slack sign-in in Mobster: a Slack destination under **Send results** posts with it, and signing out here stops that destination too. Discord, Notion and Google Sheets destinations sign in with Mobster's apps the same way ([Connect with one sign-in](/webhooks#connect-with-one-sign-in)).

### Notion

Searches and reads your pages and databases, and adds or updates pages. Click **+**, then sign in to Notion in your browser. On Enterprise, an admin who allows MCP clients **Only from approved list** blocks every client that isn't on it ([Notion MCP](https://www.notion.com/help/notion-mcp)).

### Linear

Finds, creates and updates issues, projects and comments. Click **+**, then sign in to Linear in your browser.

### GitHub

Reads repositories, issues and pull requests, and opens or comments on them. Click **+**, then sign in to GitHub in your browser, with Mobster's GitHub app. In a Copilot Business or Enterprise organization, an admin turns on the **MCP servers in Copilot** policy. An organization that restricts OAuth apps needs an admin to approve Mobster's GitHub app before its repositories show, and Enterprise Managed Users have personal access tokens off unless an admin allows them ([GitHub's setup](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/how-tos/provide-context/use-mcp/set-up-the-github-mcp-server)).

### Slack

Searches your messages, lists your channels, reads a channel, a DM or a thread, and sends messages as you. Click **+**, then sign in to Slack in your browser, with Mobster's Slack app. A workspace that restricts apps needs an admin to approve Mobster's Slack app, or yours.

Mobster answers Slack's five tools itself, on Slack's Web API with your sign-in, so they work with any app your workspace installed: Slack's MCP server takes only Marketplace and internal apps. Each tool needs the user scope Slack asked you to allow:

| Tool | What it does | Slack's method | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `search_messages` | Searches public and private channels, group DMs and DMs, newest first. Slack's search words work, such as `from:@sam in:#team after:2026-10-01` | `assistant.search.context` | `search:read.public`, `search:read.private`, `search:read.mpim`, `search:read.im` |
| `list_channels` | The public and private channels you're in, with their ids | `users.conversations` | `channels:read`, `groups:read` |
| `read_channel` | A channel's or DM's latest messages, by id or `#name` | `conversations.history` | `channels:history`, `groups:history`, `mpim:history`, `im:history` |
| `read_thread` | A thread's messages, from its first message's `ts` | `conversations.replies` | the same history scopes |
| `send_message` | Posts as you in a channel or DM, or replies in a thread. `&`, `<` and `>` are escaped, so text from a screen can't mention `@channel` or a person | `chat.postMessage` | `chat:write` |

A post that went out and got no answer, or a `5xx`, says `The message may have posted: check Slack before you send it again.` and is never sent again. A sign-in without a tool's scope says which one, such as `Your Slack sign-in can't do this: it needs chat:write.` Sign in again and allow it.

### Google Drive

Searches and reads the Google Drive files you pick, and the files Mobster made. Click **+**, then sign in to Google in your browser, with Mobster's Google app ([Google's setup](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/configure-mcp-server)). Google asks to let Mobster see, edit, create and delete only the Drive files you use with Mobster (`drive.file`). Mobster never sees your other files.

After you allow Mobster, the same tab opens Google's file picker over a page titled `Choose files for Mobster`. Pick the files Mobster may open, then choose **Select**. The page says `Mobster can open 3 files` and hands you back to Mobster. To pick more later, choose **Details** on Google Drive's row, then **Choose files**. Mobster keeps no list of what you picked: Google does, and you take Mobster's access back in your Google Account at [myaccount.google.com/connections](https://myaccount.google.com/connections).

- The picker is Google's own, on a page your Mac serves at `http://127.0.0.1`. Your Google sign-in goes from your Mac's Keychain to that page and from there to Google, never to Mobster's servers. The page opens once: to open it again, choose **Choose files** again.
- A task that asks for a file you didn't pick gets `not found` from Google Drive, and Mobster's agent asks you to pick that file with **Choose files**.
- **Choose files** needs Mobster's own Google app. With your own app it says `Choose files works with Mobster's own Google app, and this Google Drive signs in with yours. Remove Google Drive, then connect it again without your own app.`, and Mobster reads only the files it made.
- Until Mobster's picker key is set up, **Choose files** says `Mobster's Google Drive file picker isn't set up yet, so Choose files can't open. Try again later.`, and the sign-in ends on the `Connected to Google Drive` tab.

### Sentry

Searches issues and errors, reads their events and stack traces, and updates issues. Click **+**, then sign in to Sentry and allow the scopes it lists: `org:read`, `project:write`, `team:write`, `event:write` and `alerts:write`. It reaches organizations on sentry.io, not a self-hosted Sentry ([Sentry MCP](https://mcp.sentry.dev/)).

Try: `Find the newest crash in Sentry for my app and repeat its steps on my iPhone`.

### Expo

Lists and starts EAS builds and workflows, reads their logs, and reads TestFlight crashes and App Store reviews. Click **+**, then sign in to Expo. `search_documentation` needs an EAS paid plan, and `build_run` a GitHub repository connected to the project ([Expo MCP](https://docs.expo.dev/mcp/)). Expo's tools can also submit builds to the stores and reply to reviews; each asks you first until you allow it.

Try: `Read the newest TestFlight crashes in Expo and repeat each one on my iPhone`.

### GitLab

Finds and creates issues and merge requests, comments on them, and checks pipelines. Click **+**, then sign in to GitLab.com with Mobster's own GitLab app; GitLab's consent page names Mobster and asks for the `mcp` scope. If Mobster's app isn't set up, **+** says so and offers **Use your own app**. First, an Owner of your top-level group turns on access to GitLab's MCP server for the group. The `duo_agent_platform` tools need a GitLab Duo seat ([GitLab's MCP server](https://docs.gitlab.com/user/model_context_protocol/mcp_server/)).

For a self-managed GitLab, choose **Use your own GitLab** beside the server address on GitLab's page, type your server's address, such as `git.example.com`, and sign in there; your server registers Mobster itself, because Mobster's GitLab app lives on GitLab.com only. Mobster connects to `https://<your GitLab>/api/v4/mcp` and refuses an address that starts with `http://` or has a path. An administrator turns on **Allow access to the MCP server** for the instance first.

Try: `Test the merge request's change on my iPhone and comment the result in GitLab`.

### Jira

Searches, creates and updates Jira issues and Confluence pages, and comments on them, through Atlassian's server. Click **+**, then sign in to Atlassian and choose your site. Mobster asks for every scope the server lists, and Atlassian's consent page shows them. Calls use your organization's Rovo credits ([Atlassian's guide](https://support.atlassian.com/atlassian-ai-gateway/docs/get-started-with-the-atlassian-remote-mcp-server/)).

Try: `File a Jira bug with the steps that broke on my iPhone`.

### Airtable

Searches your bases, reads their records, and creates or updates records. Click **+**, then sign in to Airtable. It works on every Airtable plan, and calls count toward Airtable's API rate limits. On Enterprise, an admin who restricts third-party integrations allows Mobster under **Admin Panel › Settings › Integrations & development › Third party integration allowlist** ([Airtable's guide](https://support.airtable.com/articles/9897799762-using-the-airtable-mcp-server)).

Try: `Read today's posts in my Airtable content calendar and post each one from my iPhone`.

### ClickUp

Finds, creates and updates tasks, searches docs and comments, and tracks time. Click **+**, then sign in to ClickUp. Without ClickUp's Everything AI add-on, each Workspace gets a number of calls per rolling 24 hours: 100 on Free Forever, 300 on Unlimited, 1,000 on Business, 2,500 on Business Plus and 5,000 on Enterprise (ClickUp, 15 Oct 2026, [ClickUp's guide](https://developer.clickup.com/docs/connect-an-ai-assistant-to-clickups-mcp-server)). ClickUp gives Mobster no refresh token, so when ClickUp ends the sign-in, the row says `Signed out by the service. Connect to sign in again.` Click **Connect**.

Try: `Read my ClickUp tasks due today and reply to each client's DM on my iPhone`.

### Zapier

Runs actions in the apps your Zapier account connects, such as adding a spreadsheet row or a CRM contact. Click **+**, then sign in to Zapier. Zapier sets up your server as you sign in and turns on actions for the apps your Zapier already connects; turn on more at [mcp.zapier.com](https://mcp.zapier.com). Each call that works uses 2 tasks from your Zapier plan ([Zapier's guide](https://docs.zapier.com/mcp/get-started/quickstart)). To start a Zap with a finished task's result, use [Send results to other tools](/webhooks).

Try: `Read the new DM on my iPhone and add the lead to my CRM through Zapier`.

### Make

Runs your Make scenarios with the inputs they ask for, and reads what they return. Click **+**, then sign in to Make and choose the scopes on its consent page. Make waits 25 seconds for a scenario's answer; a longer scenario keeps running in Make for up to 40 minutes, and the agent can read its output afterwards with `scenarios:read`. Running scenarios works on every Make plan; the tools that change scenarios need a paid plan ([Make's MCP server](https://developers.make.com/mcp-server)).

Try: `Run my Make scenario New lead with the name and email from this DM on my iPhone`.

### monday.com

Reads your boards, creates and updates items and their columns, and posts updates. Click **+**, then sign in to monday.com with Mobster's own monday.com app; monday.com's consent page names Mobster and lists what it asks for: your profile, boards, workspaces, users and updates. Mobster lists monday.com only while its monday.com app is set up. If it isn't, **Connect** says `Mobster's own monday.com app isn't set up yet, so it isn't offered yet. Try again later`, and monday.com is hidden. Calls count toward your account's daily API limit ([monday.com's MCP](https://developer.monday.com/api-reference/docs/mondaycom-mcp)).

Try: `Add each new client DM on my iPhone as an item on my monday.com board Leads`.

### Microsoft 365

Reads your Outlook mail, calendar, OneDrive files, contacts and Teams chats, and sends email and chat messages and adds calendar events. Click **+**, then sign in with a work, school or personal Microsoft account; Microsoft's consent page names Mobster and lists what it asks for: your profile, mail (read, write and send), calendars, files, contacts and chats. Microsoft 365 has no MCP server that Mobster's app can sign in to, so Mobster runs the tools itself on Microsoft Graph with your sign-in: `list_mail`, `read_message`, `list_events`, `search_files`, `list_contacts`, `list_chats` and `read_chat` read; `send_mail`, `send_chat_message` and `create_event` write.

- The three writes always ask first. Whatever mode you choose for them under the connector's tools, and whatever the run's access, Mobster shows the recipients and the text on the approval card and acts only when you approve. Mobster sends plain text only. A send whose outcome Mobster doesn't know, such as an email Microsoft didn't answer, is never sent again: Mobster says so and you check Outlook.
- A work or school account whose admin hasn't allowed the app stops at Microsoft's consent page. Ask your admin to allow Mobster, or use a personal account.
- If Microsoft says a call is `refused` with a 403, your sign-in lacks that permission: **Connect** again and allow what Microsoft asks.
- Mobster lists Microsoft 365 only while its Microsoft app is set up. If it isn't, **Connect** says `Mobster's own Microsoft 365 app isn't set up yet, so it isn't offered yet. Try again later`, and Microsoft 365 is hidden.

Try: `Read the confirmation email in Outlook and add the trip to the Calendar on my iPhone`.

### Bitrise

Write access adds payment and transfer requests, which still need approval in Mercury, and transaction notes and categories, which apply at once; each asks you first until you allow it ([Mercury's guide](https://docs.mercury.com/docs/connecting-mercury-mcp)).

Try: `Check Mercury for yesterday's transactions and add any missing receipts from Photos on my iPhone to my notes`.

### Ramp

Queries your company's spend, transactions and cards, and acts on Ramp as the user who signs in. Click **+**, then sign in to Ramp. It never goes past that user's role, and admin controls and scopes narrow it further. An answer holds up to 100 rows, and it can't upload or download files, so receipts go through the Ramp app (Ramp, 15 Oct 2026, [Ramp's MCP overview](https://agents.ramp.com/docs/mcp/overview)).

Try: `Check Ramp for transactions missing receipts and open each receipt email in Mail on my iPhone`.
### Calendly
Finds open times and your meetings, creates single-use booking links, and books or cancels meetings. Click **+**, then sign in to Calendly. Calendly takes no `127.0.0.1` address, so your browser comes back to Mobster at `http://localhost`, on the same listener. Booking a meeting for someone needs a paid Calendly plan, and routing forms need Teams or higher (Calendly, 15 Oct 2026, [Calendly's MCP server](https://developer.calendly.com/calendly-mcp-server)).
Try: `Send a single-use Calendly link to the client who just DMed me on my iPhone`.

### Add your own server

Choose **Add a server** under **Your own server**:

- **URL**: a streamable HTTP MCP server. On the internet, the URL starts with `https://`. `http://` works for `localhost` and `127.0.0.1`. Paste a token if the server takes one; leave it empty to sign in with your browser, when the server supports OAuth.
- **Command**: a server Mobster starts on this Mac, such as `npx -y @notionhq/notion-mcp-server`. Put its keys under **Environment**, one `NAME=value` a line. The server starts with `PATH`, `HOME`, `LANG`, `USER`, `TMPDIR` and those values only, never your model keys. A server that runs on `npx` needs Node.js from [nodejs.org](https://nodejs.org); without it, **Test** says `Mobster couldn't find npx`.

Add starts the command and runs the MCP handshake before it saves anything. A command that isn't found, stops or answers in something else (`/bin/echo hello`) isn't saved: Mobster says what it saw and `Mobster saved nothing.` A command still starting after 9 seconds, such as a first `npx` download, is saved, and **Test** tries it again.

Choose **Test** on the connector's row to connect again and read its tools.

To fix your own server's address, choose **Edit** on its page. The form opens in place with its name and its URL or command, and **Save** connects to the new address. A token or environment values you type replace the saved ones; left empty, the saved ones stay. A URL on another host never gets the old server's token or OAuth sign-in: paste that server's token, or connect to sign in again. A command must answer MCP before it's saved, as when you add one. A connector from the directory or from a plugin keeps its address, so its page has no **Edit**.

### Import servers from your other apps

When Claude Desktop, Claude Code, Cursor or Codex on this Mac has MCP servers of its own, **Add a connector** shows a row such as **From Claude Desktop and Cursor**: `3 servers you already set up there.` Choose **Import**. The list opens under the row, and each server shows its command or URL. **Add** makes it a connector:

- A command's environment values, and a URL's `Authorization: Bearer` token, go to your Mac's Keychain. The list shows their names only, such as `Keeps GITHUB_PERSONAL_ACCESS_TOKEN in your Keychain.`, and a command's arguments with secrets masked.
- Mobster reads `~/Library/Application Support/Claude/claude_desktop_config.json`, the user-scope servers in `~/.claude.json`, `~/.cursor/mcp.json` and `~/.codex/config.toml`. It never writes to them, and a project's own servers stay with the project.
- Mobster itself isn't listed.
- A server that uses SSE, the older MCP transport, is listed with why it can't be added and no **Add**. Use the server's streamable HTTP URL, often ending in `/mcp`, with **Add a server** instead.
- Every tool of an imported server asks first, like any other connector's.

## Choose what each tool may do

Choose **Details** on a connector's row. Its tools are listed read-only ones first, each with three settings:

| Setting | What the agent does |
|---|---|
| **Allow** | Calls it without asking |
| **Ask** | Shows the approval card with the tool and what it sends, such as `Use Linear: create_issue? {"title": "Fix login"}`, and calls it only after you approve. It asks with **Full access** too |
| **Block** | Never sees it |

Every tool starts at **Ask**, and so does a tool a server adds later. Some servers mark a tool read-only, and Settings lists those first; the label is the server's own, and the tool still asks until you choose **Allow**. A change applies at once, also to a task that's running.

The switch on a connector's row turns the whole connector on or off.

## What happens in a task

When a task starts, the agent gets the list of tools it may use, with each tool's description, up to 30 tools. Tools of a service your request names come first, then the tools your request is most about: a word of your request in a tool's name counts more than one in its description. The agent calls one as `CALL_TOOL linear.search_issues {"query": "login"}`.

With more than 30 tools, the list ends with `and 51 more tools not listed here`, and the agent searches the rest with `CALL_TOOL tools.search {"query": "create an invoice"}`. The search runs on your Mac and sends nothing to any server, so it never asks. It answers with the 8 best matches, and the task's steps show it as `Looked for a connected tool: create an invoice`. A tool it finds is called like a listed one, and asks the same way. Because of the search, a connector can't be named Tools.

The list shows each tool's arguments one level deep, such as `pages?: array of object`. Before a call whose arguments hold objects, such as a Notion page or an Airtable record, the agent searches for the tool's exact name, `CALL_TOOL tools.search {"query": "notion.notion-create-pages"}`, and gets the tool's whole description and its arguments as JSON Schema. Mobster keeps a tool's schema whole up to 24,000 characters; a bigger one loses its descriptions first, and one still too big keeps only its top level.

- The call is written to the task's history before it is sent, as a `connector_call` event with the connector, the tool and its arguments (secrets masked).
- The task's steps show it as `Used Linear: search_issues`, and the live line as `Using Linear: search_issues`.
- What the tool returns reaches the agent as data, never as instructions: a page that says "ignore your instructions" is read, never followed. Card numbers, codes and keys in it are masked, and it's cut at 6,000 characters. Images in an answer are left out.
- A call times out after 60 seconds. Then the step says `Linear: search_issues didn't answer`, and Mobster never sends that call again in the task: it may or may not have happened, so the agent checks another way.
- Mobster reconnects once, and sends the call once, when the session stopped answering before the call went out: a command that stopped, a server that restarted and forgot the session (HTTP `404`), or a `401` because a sign-in expired, after Mobster refreshes it. In each case nothing ran on the server. It never resends a call that went out without an answer, or one the server answered with an error.
- A server that says its tools changed (`notifications/tools/list_changed`) has them read again before the next call.

### The events a call writes

Each call writes `connector_call` events to the task's history, which `GET /api/runs/{id}` and its event stream return:

```json
{"event": "connector_call", "connector": "Linear", "connectorId": "3f9a0c21b7de", "tool": "create_issue", "mode": "ask", "status": "sending", "arguments": "{\"title\": \"Fix login\"}"}
{"event": "connector_call", "connector": "Linear", "connectorId": "3f9a0c21b7de", "tool": "create_issue", "mode": "ask", "status": "done", "ms": 412, "excerpt": "Created ENG-431: Fix login"}
```

`status` is `sending` before the call goes out, then `done`, `error` (the server said why, in `message`), `not_sent` (nothing reached the server) or `unknown` (no answer came). A call Mobster refused before sending has only `refused`. A tool search writes one event, `{"event": "connector_call", "connector": "Connected services", "tool": "search", "status": "searched", "arguments": "create an invoice", "excerpt": "books.create_invoice, books.list_customers"}`. `arguments` and `excerpt` are masked like everything else in the history. Slack's answers keep no `excerpt`: Slack asks the apps that read it to keep none of its data.

A workflow's **Use a connector** step calls one tool by itself, with no model: an allowed tool runs, and a tool that asks or is blocked stops the workflow before anything is sent ([The graph](/workflows#the-graph)).

A workflow or a task can name the connectors it may use with the `connectors` field of [`POST /api/runs`](/scripting), as names or ids. Without it, a task may use every connector that's on.

## Use Mobster from Claude and ChatGPT

**Use Mobster from…** ends the Connectors page. It connects the other way: your other assistants and automations hand tasks to your iPhone. It has two groups: **Through Mobster Remote** and **On this Mac**.

- **Through Mobster Remote** says once whether Remote is on. With it off, the group's head has **Set up Remote**. **Claude** and **ChatGPT** each have a row. Once Claude is connected through [Mobster Remote](/remote), its row says `Connected. Ask Claude to use your iPhone, on the web, on your Mac and on your phone.` Otherwise the row says where to paste the URL, such as `In Claude, Customize › Connectors › Add custom connector, then paste the URL.`, and with Remote on it has **Copy URL** and **Steps** ([Assistants](/remote/assistants)).
- **Zapier** has a row too. It signs in to Mobster from a Zap, so the row says where, `Accept Mobster’s Zapier invite, then in a Zap choose Mobster as a step’s app and Sign in.`, with **Add to Zapier** (Mobster's Zapier invite: the app is private until Zapier lists it) and **Steps**, and no URL to copy ([Zapier](/zapier)). Make has no row: Mobster's Make app is private to its makers until Make shares it. A scenario can start a workflow with its webhook URL instead ([Start a workflow from another tool](/webhooks#start-a-workflow-from-another-tool)).
- **On this Mac**: **Claude Code**, **Claude Desktop**, **Cursor** and **Codex** each have **Add**, which writes Mobster's local MCP server into that app's settings, and **Remove** ([Add it from the Mac app](/mcp-server#add-it-from-the-mac-app)). Mobster's MCP server is tested with Claude Code and Codex. An agent set up with another Mobster install says `Uses another Mobster install. Switch to this one.`, with **Switch**. **Another agent** has the command for any other client, and **Siri and Shortcuts** links to its setup.

## Where your sign-in is kept

Signing in to a server with a URL uses OAuth 2.1 the way the MCP authorization spec says: Mobster reads the server's protected-resource metadata (from its `401`, else `/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource`), then its authorization server's metadata, registers itself as a client when the server allows that (else, when the server reads client metadata from a URL, uses `https://mobster.dev/oauth/client-metadata.json` as its client ID), and signs in with PKCE (`S256`) through your browser and a listener on `127.0.0.1`. It refreshes the token before it expires, and after a `401`.

Tokens, OAuth sign-ins, client secrets and environment values are kept in your Mac's login Keychain, under `app.mobster.desktop.connectors`. Mobster shows a server's scheme, host and path only, and never writes a token in its logs, a task's history or an answer from its API. **Disconnect** and **Remove**, under **Details**, delete the Keychain item and cancel a pending sign-in, then ask the service to revoke the sign-in when its sign-in server offers revocation (RFC 7009), as Notion, Linear, Sentry, Expo, Jira, Zapier, GitLab, Make and Canva do. Mobster sends the token only to a revocation address on the same origin as the service's token address. A service that offers no revocation, or doesn't answer within 5 seconds, keeps the access you gave Mobster until you remove it in that service's own settings, such as Airtable's or ClickUp's list of connected apps. Mobster's own Slack, Google and GitHub apps don't revoke yet. Each of **Disconnect** and **Remove** asks first, where its button was (`Disconnect Linear?`, `Remove Linear?`), with **Cancel** beside it. A sign-in callback or token refresh already in progress cannot restore credentials after you disconnect, remove the connector or replace its token.

## What is sent

A tool call sends what the agent passes the tool, such as your search words or an issue's title, to that server, under that vendor's terms. Nothing else goes to it: never a screenshot, your conversation or your memory, unless the agent puts some of it in the call's arguments. [Privacy](/privacy) lists every place Mobster sends data.

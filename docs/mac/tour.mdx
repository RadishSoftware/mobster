Mobster for Mac is one window: a sidebar on the left, the task page or a conversation in the middle, and your iPhone's screen on the right. The app has its own 1-minute tour too: Help › **Take the tour**.

1. **New task** (⌘N) starts a new conversation.
2. **Search** (⌘K) finds conversations, tasks, workflows and apps.
3. **Workflows** (⌘2) and **Apps** (⌘3). **Needs you** shows above them while a task waits for you. ⌘1, or the Mobster mark at the top, goes **Home**.
4. **Your conversations**, newest first, grouped by day. A dot says one is running, waiting for you, or worth a look.
5. **The task box.** Type a task. **+** attaches files, pins an app (typing `@` in the task pins one too) or opens Connectors to connect a tool. The rest sits beside **+** in the box: who does the task, the profile, the app and phone it runs on, **⋯** for the answer's format and when Mobster asks first, where the result goes, and what the task costs.
6. **Starters** fill the task box with a task to try. They lead with the jobs for what you hand off (Settings › General): **Check my build** for your app, **Clear DM backlog** for your accounts, and one of each until you choose.
7. **Help** has [Send feedback](/feedback), the tour, help with your iPhone's setup, and support. **Settings** (⌘,) sits above it.

## Conversations

Each task starts a conversation. Mobster shows each step as it goes, and the result as an answer with the screen it came from. Follow up in the same box, and the next task knows what the last one found. [Conversations](/conversations) has the details.

## Approvals and questions

Before anything that sends, buys, posts or deletes, a card shows the exact action and text, with the act's own verb. The card takes the task box's place until you answer, so its **Say what to do instead** is where you change the plan. When Mobster presses a spot on the screen, **Show the screen** under the text shows that spot. When a task could mean two things, Mobster may ask you which one, with each choice as a button. [Approvals and safety](/safety) has the rules.

## The phone pane

On the right, your iPhone's screen, live, while Mobster can see it. The phone's name at the top picks another phone when you have several, the hand button lets you tap and type on the phone yourself, the cap teaches Mobster a task you do once ([Teach Mobster a task](/capabilities#teach-mobster-a-task)), and the arrows give the screen more room. When the phone needs something, such as a refresh of Mobster's helper or an unlock, the pane says what to do and offers the one button that does it.

After you turn on Control, pressing Home keeps it on through a brief interruption. Input waits while the phone cannot answer. After 3 seconds away, Control turns off. Switching phones also turns Control off and closes the previous phone's save form.

## Search

Press ⌘K to search your conversations, tasks and apps. Before you type, the current page's commands appear above your newest conversations. Once you type, matching conversations appear first. Use ↑ and ↓ to move, Enter to open a result, and Esc to close search.

Type `new` or `add` to find **New task**, **New workflow** and **Add a device**.

## Your conversations and workflows

At the top of the sidebar are **New task**, **Search**, **Needs you** and **Workflows**. **Needs you** shows a count when something is waiting.

**Projects** and **Profiles** list your items, with the one you use most first. Choose **+** to add one. Empty sections stay hidden.

**Recent** shows your newest 20 conversations, one per row. A dot marks a task that is running or needs you. A separate mark identifies a task that couldn't finish or stopped without you. Other rows show only the title. Hover over a row to see when it started. Choose **View all** to search every task in ⌘K.

The device chip at the bottom shows where your next task will run. Its dot shows the device's state; text appears when the state changes. Open its menu to switch devices, **Add a device** or **Manage devices**.

Devices, Apps, Profiles, Connectors and Memory open in Settings, with the sections on the left. **Back** at the top of the sections returns to where you were before Settings.

Each conversation's **More** menu contains **Run workflow here…**, **Rename** and **Delete**.

**All conversations**, in ⌘K, opens every conversation on this Mac, newest first, with a search box beside the title. Each row shows the app, the title, whether it's **Running**, **Needs you** or **Couldn't finish**, which phone when more than one is paired, and when it last changed. **Show older conversations** loads the next 100.

**Workflows** (⌘2) holds conversations you start again. A workflow is plain-text steps with blanks for what changes, such as `Scroll {topic} Reels for {10} minutes and tell me what you learned`. You write the steps the way you'd type a task, and the agent works out the taps.

- Choose **⋯** under an answer, then **Save as workflow**. A new workflow opens with a draft name and steps from the conversation.
- **Say what it does**, at the top of the Workflows page, and **Make it** opens a new workflow's page with the steps Mobster drafts from your words, to read and save. **Teach it** opens your iPhone to do the task once while Mobster watches, and the starters under it, until you have three workflows, are for apps your iPhone has. **New workflow** starts from an empty one.
- **Now**, above your workflows when there is any, lists what is going and what waits for you: a workflow running its steps (**Stop**) and an approval (**Approve** or **Decline** in place, or **Review** on its step when you can change it first or should see the screen). A workflow whose last run failed leads the list, with why.
- Each row shows the app it works in, what its last run found, and how it starts. Once it has at least two runs, point at its status for a summary of up to 10 recent runs. After what needs you, the rows that run next come first, and rows keep their order while the pointer or focus is on the list.
- **Run**, the play button at a row's end, asks for the blanks, then starts a new conversation. A workflow also runs from ⌘K, and from the triggers on its canvas: a schedule, a webhook or a shortcut.
- A workflow's page is a canvas: double-click or type to add a step (a phone task, a wait, a send, a connector's tool, a branch, a loop and more), and drag from a step's output to another step to join them. **Run** tests it there and lights each step as it goes. The list button on its row shows each run, with **Stop** and **Export CSV** ([Build a workflow on its canvas](/workflows)).
- **Learned steps**, folded under your workflows, lists what Mobster repeats with no model: tasks you taught and tasks it kept after they finished. Each has **Run**, **Rename** and **Forget**.

[Workflows and schedules](/schedules) has the details.

## Share a task

**Save as GIF**, in the **⋯** at the end of the icons under a finished task's answer, saves it to Downloads as a captioned GIF you can post: the task as you typed it, each step's screen with what Mobster did, the approval if it asked, and the result. It matches the app's appearance, light or dark, and stays under X's 15 MB limit. The GIF shows every screen of the task and stays on your Mac until you share it, so look at it first. Screens after a sign-in code are left out. To blur text fields and messages, or to save an MP4, use [`mobster export`](/reference/cli#mobster-export) with `--redact` or `--mp4`.

**Send to…** and **File as issue…** are in the same **⋯**. Send to lists the places you saved in Connectors, such as a Slack channel, a webhook, a sheet or a folder, under the result. Pick one and press **Send**. The line under the result says when it arrived, or why it didn't, with **Resend**.

File as issue files the result in the GitHub, Linear or Jira you connected in Connectors. The first time, you name where it goes: the repository as owner/name, the team, or the Jira site and project key. Mobster remembers it on this Mac. The issue gets the answer, how the task ended and the device. Screenshots stay on your Mac. When the tracker doesn't answer, Mobster says it couldn't confirm the issue and never files it again by itself, so look in the tracker first.

## Tests

A project's page, under **Projects** in the sidebar, holds its tests: **Run checks**, its **Checks** (each one's last 20 results on each device as dots, and its pass rate) and its **Last runs** by branch, where a check that didn't pass shows why, its log and its screen. The old Test results address, `/tests`, opens **Projects**.

With `MOBSTER_EXPERIMENTAL_TESTS_PAGE=1` in the agent's environment, the page adds, for that project only:

- A menu beside **Run checks** that picks a simulator from `.mobster/matrix.yaml` or a plugged-in iPhone, and the checks with one tag.
- **Heals to review**, above the checks, when a step healed: each step before and after, its two frames (click one to show it in Finder), and **Accept** or **Keep failing** ([Replay and heals](/testing#replay-and-heals)).
- On each check's row, its flake score and its run times, with the newest one and whether it got slower or faster.
- **Schedules**: each schedule's next run and last result, **Run now**, and a switch that turns it off on this Mac only.
- **Deep links**: each link with the title its screen should show and its last check. **Check links** checks them all on a simulator, as the project's run.
- **Fixtures**: what each one sets up, **Simulators only** when it changes the device's state, and **Apply**, which puts the app on Mobster's simulator in that state now. A fixture that changes the device's state asks before it applies.
- **Snapshots**, when a screen differs from its baseline or has none yet.

## Settings

Settings (⌘,) is a page, with its sections on the left and **Back** to where you were. It holds Models and your spending limits, Safety, Connectors, Plugins, Memory, Voice, Remote, Devices, Profiles, your account, Help and support, Advanced, and For developers. [Settings](/mac/settings) goes through each one.

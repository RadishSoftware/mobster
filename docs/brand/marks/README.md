# Partner and company marks

The one source for every mark of another company that Mobster shows: the agents, assistants and AI models it works with
(Claude, ChatGPT, Codex, Cursor, Gemini and the rest), the services it connects to (every connector, destination and
featured plugin: Slack, Notion, GitHub, Google Drive, Linear, Jira, Stripe and the rest), and the companies Network
X-ray names beside the domains an app contacted (Google, Meta, Amazon and the rest; `companies` in `marks.json`). Each
partner's mark only names the product Mobster works with, as its owner's brand guidelines allow for an integration.
Each compared tool's mark (Maestro, Appium, BrowserStack, Firebase, Sauce Labs, TapKit, Xcode for XCUITest) only names the tool on its comparison page and that page's cover, beside Mobster's own mark, with the page's line that Mobster isn't affiliated with or endorsed by its owner. A tool whose owner's terms forbid it, or don't cover it, has no mark here. Each company's mark only says whose servers they are; a company with no mark here keeps its name and no mark. It's never changed, never recoloured (a one-colour mark takes the page's ink, as
its maker's own light and dark versions do), never larger than Mobster's own mark, and never shown in a way that
suggests a partnership.

- `svg/`: each partner's own artwork, from its press kit, brand page, app or website, unchanged but for editor metadata,
  a wordmark cut away from its symbol, and, where a file set its colours with a style sheet, the same colours as
  attributes; never a third party's copy, and never drawn, traced or generated. A company mark only Network X-ray shows
  is Simple Icons 13.21.0 (CC0 1.0).
- `marks.json`: each mark's file, whose it is, where it came from and when, whether it is one colour (`ink`) or an
  icon that is its own tile (`fill`), the surfaces that show it, the other names it goes by (a client's key, a
  provider's id, a destination's kind), the companies it stands for in Network X-ray (`companies`, written to
  `COMPANY_MARKS` in the app's list), and its box and optical scale.
- [Sources](#sources), below: the same list as a table, written from `marks.json`.

## One tile

Every surface draws a mark the same way:

- **The tile** is the raised surface (`--surface`: white on Paper, `#1A191F` on Night) with the app icon's corner
  radius (23%) and ring (`--shadow-app-icon` in the app; the same hairline on the site and Remote's pages).
- **The mark** sits in a box 9/16 of the tile (18 px in a 32 px tile, 27 px in 48), centred. Each copy is the tile's
  whole square with the mark already in its box, so a surface fills the tile with it.
- **The optical scale.** Inside that box each mark is drawn at the scale `scripts/gen_agent_marks.py --measure` found,
  so a solid mark (OpenAI's blossom, Cursor's cube) and a thin one (Claude's spark) read the same size. A logo that is
  a name (Amp's, Wix's) may run wider than the box, up to 3/4 of the tile.
- **Colour.** A one-colour mark is drawn in the page's ink through its shape (a mask), so it reads on both tiles. A
  mark in colours keeps them, with its owner's version for dark grounds where it has one.
- **An icon is its own tile.** A logo that is itself a tile (Jira's, Todoist's, Miro's, Box's), or an owner whose only
  mark readable at a tile's size is its app icon (Stripe, Runway, Paddle) or whose rules name its app icon for small
  sizes (Zapier: "the smallest expression of our logo"), fills the tile instead (`fill`): the icon
  as it is, never set inside a second tile, clipped to the app icon's corners, as an app icon is.

Without a mark (an assistant Mobster doesn't know, a custom endpoint), the same tile holds the name's first letter or a
glyph.

## Changing a mark

1. Put the maker's file in `svg/` and its line in `marks.json`: where you took it from and the date.
2. Run `python3 scripts/gen_agent_marks.py --measure` (needs `rsvg-convert` and Pillow): it measures the new mark's box
   and scale and writes every surface's copy.
3. `mobile_agent/tests/test_agent_marks.py` fails while a copy is stale, and `test_connectors.py` while a connector
   has no mark.
4. Look at it on every surface that shows it, light and dark, at each size.

| Mobster for Mac (`dashboard/`) | `public/marks/`, `src/lib/marks.gen.ts` | `src/components/ui/logo-tile.tsx` (`LogoTile`, `LogoMark`) |

## Owners' rules

Each row's `rules` links its owner's guidelines. The ones that shape how Mobster may show a mark:

- **Slack:** "Sign in with Slack" takes Slack's own button (api.slack.com/sign-in-with-slack-button-generator); its
  brand terms say most uses of its logo need a written licence (slack.com/terms-of-service/slack-brand).
- **Google (Drive, Sheets):** the product icon, resized only; Google's branding page allows it on a button that acts
  on Drive or to show a file is stored there (developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/branding). "Sign in with
  Google" buttons follow developers.google.com/identity/branding-guidelines.
- **Atlassian (Jira):** the app icon is never put inside another container and keeps its corners
  (atlassian.design/foundations/logos), so Jira's icon is its own tile (`fill`).
- **A one-colour mark in ink:** the page's ink (`#181818` on Paper, `#F2F2F2` on Night) stands in for the owner's black
  and white versions. GitHub, Linear, Expo, Resend and Vimeo ask that their files aren't recoloured at all.
- **Permission first:** Box, Basecamp (37signals), PayPal and HubSpot ask for written permission before their logos are
  used; the WordPress Foundation allows its logo to refer to a service but never in one's name.
- **PostHog:** its logo page asks integrations for the logomark at small sizes (brand.posthog.com/logo.md), while its
  brand repository is under PolyForm Strict.
